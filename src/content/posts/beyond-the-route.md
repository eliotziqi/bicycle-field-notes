*Why I Ride, Explore, and Share*

### Different Roads, Different Reasons

Back in college, many of my cycling friends were into racing. They trained regularly, traveled to nearby cities for collegiate races, and worked their way up through different categories. I joined them for a season and enjoyed the atmosphere, especially watching everyone push themselves and celebrate their results.

But I gradually realized that what I loved most about cycling was something different: **exploration**.

For me, a bicycle has always been more than a way to exercise or compete. It's a way to discover unfamiliar places, take the quieter roads, and see things I might otherwise miss.

### Making Time for Adventure

As a student, summer and winter breaks gave me the freedom to spend days, sometimes weeks, on the road. Bike touring and bikepacking became some of my favorite ways to travel. There was time to take detours, explore somewhere unexpected, or simply stop and enjoy the view.

But life changes, and so does the time available for adventures.

With internships, work, and other commitments, taking a week off isn't always realistic. That doesn't mean giving up the joy of exploring. It just means finding different ways to fit it into everyday life.

Sometimes it's a day ride through the countryside. Sometimes it's driving or taking a train somewhere new, then spending a weekend cycling and camping. And sometimes it's a small adventure close to home.

**Not every adventure needs a long vacation.**

### More Than Distance and Elevation

When sharing cycling routes, we often start with the numbers: distance, elevation gain, gradient, and perhaps the road surface.

They're useful, but they don't tell the whole story.

A beautiful mountain road might be a training climb for one rider, a scenic escape for another, or part of a longer bikepacking journey for someone else.

And a great route isn't necessarily a great trip for everyone. Getting there, finding enough time, arranging transport, and knowing what to expect can matter just as much as the ride itself.

That's why I think a cycling route is more than a line on a map. It's a collection of possibilities, shaped by where we start, what we're looking for, and how we choose to explore.

### Why Bicycle Field Notes?

I started **Bicycle Field Notes** to document the places I've explored by bike, the routes I've taken, and what I've learned along the way.

Part personal journal, part practical guide, it's an ongoing collection of places explored and adventures worth sharing.

I hope to go beyond simply posting a GPX file or a route summary. Where possible, I'd like to share what makes a place worth visiting, how to get there, what the riding is like, and what might help someone plan their own trip.

Not every post will be a detailed guide. Some will be stories, observations, or moments from the road. Others might become route comparisons, maps, or tools that make planning a little easier.

The idea is simple: **explore, document, and share what I've learned.**

### More Roads Ahead

There's still a lot I haven't explored, and plenty I don't know.

This isn't meant to be a definitive guide to cycling, or a collection of the best routes. It's a growing record of places, experiences, and discoveries, shaped by my own rides and, hopefully, by the people who find something useful here.

If these field notes help someone discover a new place, plan a weekend adventure, or simply feel inspired to get on their bike, that's already a good reason to keep writing.

**Here's to more roads, more discoveries, and making the world a little more explorable by bike.**
